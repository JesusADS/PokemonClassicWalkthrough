# How to use the guide

This guide reorganizes the *Pokémon Classic v1.5* documentation as a chronological walkthrough. Each volume covers a part of the adventure and maintains a common structure so you can refer to it as you play.

## Symbols

| Symbol | Meaning | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| 🎯 | Objective | Action needed to move forward |
| 💎 | Important item | Unique item, TM, stone, or valuable resource |
| 🔍 | Hidden | Item that is not shown as a Poké Ball |
| ⭐ | Editorial recommendation | Particularly useful capture or decision |
| ⚠ | Warning | Battle, detour, or easy-to-forget point |
| 🏆 | Boss or gym | Preparation and reward |
| ↩ | Return later | Content requiring an HM or other progress |
| 🏄 | Surf | Encounter available on the water after unlocking Surf |
| 🎣 | Fishing | Encounter requiring the indicated Old Rod, Good Rod, or Super Rod |

The symbols are used with the same meaning in all volumes. A section may not display any if it does not contain information of that type.

In water-encounter tables, the method appears next to each Pokémon. `🏄 Surf` requires Surf and the necessary Badge; `🎣` always indicates which rod to use. The `↩` symbol means that you will usually need to return after progressing through the story.

## Confirmed data and recommendations

Lists of items, encounters, maps, and Trainer names come from the source guide. Team recommendations and the order of visits are an editorial reorganization intended to make the walkthrough easier to follow.

When *Pokémon Classic* does not detail a Gym Leader's team, *Pokémon Yellow* is used as a reference because the hack reproduces much of that adventure. Documented differences always take priority, and unconfirmed data is explicitly identified.

## Structure of a zone

- Map and main objective.
- Numbered route.
- Pokémon available and recommended catches.
- Visible and hidden items, plus content for later.
- Trainers and departure checklist.

## Structure of a gym or main combat

- Confirmed maximum level or notice that the source does not document it.
- Confirmed team or stated reference; levels and moves are never invented.
- Preparation based on the available Pokémon.
- Reward and subsequent checklist.

## Essential Pokémon Classic mechanics

- It is a reinterpretation of *Pokémon Yellow* built on *Pokémon Emerald*, with an expanded history of Team Rocket.
- The Exp. Share works in the style of the sixth generation.
- DexNav allows you to search for Pokémon already seen, check their availability depending on the time and detect hidden encounters.
- The DexNav shows an IV rating from 0 to 3 stars and can display the Ability of species you have already caught.
- To approach a hidden Pokémon, move slowly while holding A. Running may scare it away.
- You can check IVs and EVs from the stats page of a Pokémon's summary; press A to switch views.
- HMs can be used when you own the corresponding item and have the required Badge.
- Dig can be used outside battle when you own its TM; Teleport requires a compatible Psychic-type Pokémon.
- The second floor of each Pokémon Center includes a Move Tutor, Move Deleter, and Trainer Shop.
- Partner Pikachu is special, does not evolve and has improved stats.
- Fully evolved Pokémon can also learn the level-up moves of their earlier stages.
- At home you can change the appearance of the protagonist between five categories.
- Registered Key Items can be assigned to L or R, and multiple field items can be used without closing the menu.
- The Cleanse Tag has an improved effect compared with the original games.
